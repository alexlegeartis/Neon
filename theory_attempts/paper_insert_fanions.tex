\section{Rank-dependent guarantees for Fanions and their hybrids}
\label{taf:section}

Let $r=\min(m,n)$, $d=mn$, and
$S_k(G)=\sum_{i=1}^k\sigma_i(G)$.
For $C_k=\{D:\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1,\ \|D\|_*\leq k\}$,
the support function is $S_k$. The exact partial polar factor
$D_k(H)=\sum_{i=1}^k u_i v_i^\top$ is a maximizer of
$\langle H,D\rangle$ over $C_k$; zero singular directions may be omitted.
For $a,b\geq0$, the hybrid directions
$aD_k(H)+bH/\|H\|_{\mathrm F}$ and
$aD_k(H)+b\eta\operatorname{sign}(H)$ have support functions
\[
 \phi_k^{\mathrm F}(G)=aS_k(G)+b\|G\|_{\mathrm F},\qquad
 \phi_k^{\mathrm S}(G)=aS_k(G)+b\eta\|G\|_{1,\mathrm{entry}}.
\]
These correspond to the Minkowski sums of $aC_k$ with a Frobenius ball
and an entrywise infinity ball, respectively. All directions are defined
as zero when $H=0$.

\begin{theorem}[All-rank convergence with imperfect gradient inputs]
\label{taf:master}
Suppose $f$ is Frobenius $L$-smooth and bounded below by $f_*$.
Write $G_t=\nabla f(X_t)$, $\Delta=f(X_0)-f_*$, and let
$X_{t+1}=X_t-\gamma_t\widetilde D_t$ with $\gamma_t\geq0$.
For a chosen family, suppose $\widetilde D_t$ is feasible in its oracle
set and has support deficit
$\delta_t=\phi_k(H_t)-\langle H_t,\widetilde D_t\rangle\geq0$.
Let $E_t=H_t-G_t$, where $H_t$ may first be positively rescaled.
Suppose $\phi_k(G_t)\geq\ell_k\|G_t\|_{\mathrm F}$ with $\ell_k>0$ and
$\|\widetilde D_t\|_{\mathrm F}\leq R_k$.
Then, for $\Gamma_T=\sum_{t<T}\gamma_t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{taf:bound}
 \frac{\sum_{t<T}\gamma_t\|G_t\|_{\mathrm F}}{\Gamma_T}
 \leq\frac{
 \Delta+\frac L2R_k^2\sum_{t<T}\gamma_t^2
 +2\sum_{t<T}\gamma_t\phi_k(E_t)
 +\sum_{t<T}\gamma_t\delta_t}{\ell_k\Gamma_T}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Feasibility and the triangle inequality for the support norm give
\[
 \langle G_t,\widetilde D_t\rangle
 \geq\phi_k(H_t)-\delta_t-\phi_k(E_t)
 \geq\phi_k(G_t)-2\phi_k(E_t)-\delta_t.
\]
Apply the descent lemma, sum over $t$, and use the lower bound on $f$.
\end{proof}

For $G_t\ne0$, assume
\[
 \frac{\|G_t\|_*}{\sqrt r\|G_t\|_{\mathrm F}}\geq\theta_0>0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\|G_t\|_{1,\mathrm{entry}}}{\sqrt d\|G_t\|_{\mathrm F}}
 \geq c_0>0
\]
along the trajectory, with the second condition needed only for S-Fanions.
The ordering of singular values gives $S_k\geq(k/r)\|G_t\|_*$, so valid
constants for every rank are
\begin{align}
 (\ell_k^{\mathrm P},R_k^{\mathrm P})
   &=\left(\frac{\theta_0 k}{\sqrt r},\sqrt k\right),\\
 (\ell_k^{\mathrm F},R_k^{\mathrm F})
   &=\left(\frac{a\theta_0 k}{\sqrt r}+b,a\sqrt k+b\right),\\
 (\ell_k^{\mathrm S},R_k^{\mathrm S})
   &=\left(\frac{a\theta_0 k}{\sqrt r}+b\eta c_0\sqrt d,
            a\sqrt k+b\eta\sqrt d\right).
\end{align}
For exact gradients and oracles, choosing
$\gamma=\sqrt{2\Delta/(LR_k^2T)}$ yields
\[
 \frac1T\sum_{t<T}\|G_t\|_{\mathrm F}
 \leq\frac{R_k}{\ell_k}\sqrt{\frac{2L\Delta}{T}}.
\]
For plain Fanions the squared factor is $r/(\theta_0^2k)$.
At a common fixed learning rate, their bound is instead
\[
 \frac1T\sum_{t<T}\|G_t\|_{\mathrm F}
 \leq\frac{\sqrt r}{\theta_0}
 \left(\frac{\Delta}{k\gamma T}+\frac{L\gamma}{2}\right).
\]
Thus the rank explicitly changes finite-time guarantees despite a shared
asymptotic exponent. In the hybrids, the two projection terms become
comparable near $k=(b/a)\sqrt r/\theta_0$ for F-Fanions and
$k=(b/a)\eta c_0\sqrt{rd}/\theta_0$ for S-Fanions, when $a>0$.
The curvature radii change too, so these scales alone do not establish a
universal ordering of optimally tuned ranks.

\paragraph{A finite-step example attaining the $kT$ scale.}
For $f(X)=\|X\|_{\mathrm F}^2/2$, $X_0=sI_r$, take
$\gamma=s/h$ with $h$ a positive integer. A diagonal Fanion-$k$ tie choice
always serves the $k$ largest positive residuals, stopping at zero.
Writing $\min(kT,rh)=qr+p$, $0\leq p<r$, gives exactly
\[
 f(X_T)=\frac12\left[(r-p)(s-q\gamma)^2
                         +p(s-(q+1)\gamma)^2\right].
\]
Before extinction this equals
$\frac r2(s-k\gamma T/r)^2+\frac{\gamma^2p}{2}(1-p/r)$;
the second term is at most $r\gamma^2/8$.
Hence all ranks share an approximately identical curve under the time
change $T\mapsto kT$ on this example.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The bound uses additive updates and feasible oracle directions. Finite
Newton--Schulz approximations, weight normalization, and the gradient
tracking error of a particular momentum convention must be accounted for
before applying it to a practical implementation. Gradient norm convergence
does not by itself imply a validation-accuracy ordering.
