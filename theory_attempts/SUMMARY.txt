Research summary -- 22 October 2026
==================================

Question
--------
Can convergence theory explain the observed difference between Muon,
low-rank Fanions, F-Fanions, and S-Fanions, despite their shared worst-case
convergence exponents?

Conclusion
----------
We have rigorous rank-dependent mechanisms and strong supporting evidence
for the saved quadratic experiments. For CIFAR/LLMs we have a precise,
conditional curvature explanation and a description of the actual training
geometry. The required neural-network structure has not yet been measured.
The general convergence theorem alone does not establish Muon's superiority.

What the families do
--------------------
For a matrix oracle input H with singular vectors u_i,v_i, let
  D_k(H) = sum_{i<=k} u_i v_i^T,   r = min(matrix dimensions).
Fanion-k uses D_k; Neon is k=1 and ideal Muon is k=r.
F-Fanion-k uses a D_k + b H/||H||_F.
S-Fanion-k uses a D_k + b eta sign(H), with coordinatewise sign.
For convex mixtures a,b >= 0; usually a=b=1/2.

Low-rank Fanions omit singular modes at each step. The normalized-gradient
and sign components can move directions omitted by the low-rank part.
Their benefit depends on signal alignment, curvature, component scaling,
and (for sign) the entry basis. No universal ranking across these families
or monotone hybrid ranking in k is claimed. Implemented Muon uses a finite
polynomial spectral filter rather than an exact full polar factor.

1. Why norm-agnostic theorems do not settle the comparison
--------------------------------------------------------
The same theorem form or O(epsilon^-4) exponent need not mean identical
constants: smoothness, noise, and conversion between norms can differ.
Where a theorem uses the selected dual norm to measure stationarity, the
same numerical epsilon also represents a different stopping criterion.

After replacing all geometry by worst-case estimates, the resulting bounds
can genuinely be identical. Such a guarantee remains valid but cannot
explain an empirical ordering. A common stopping metric and additional
structure are required for a discriminating specialization.

For example, under a common Frobenius curvature bound, ideal Fanion-k has
direction quality
  q_k(G) = (sum_{i<=k} sigma_i(G))^2 / (k ||G||_F^2).
At a flat full-rank spectrum, q_Neon=1/r and q_Muon=1. At an almost
rank-one full-rank spectrum, the comparison reverses. Both endpoints have
worst-case infimum 1/r. Worst-case constants can therefore erase a real
problem-dependent advantage.

2. Rigorous results across Fanion ranks and hybrids
--------------------------------------------------
The reports establish the following with explicit assumptions and proofs.

  * For the manuscript's quadratic f(X)=||sqrt(M)X sqrt(N)||_F^2/2,
    let E0=sqrt(M)X0 sqrt(N), L=||M||op ||N||op, and Gamma_T=sum gamma_t.
    Every additive rank-k trajectory with ideal per-step operator norm at
    most one satisfies
      f(X_T) >= 1/2 sum_{j>kT} sigma_j(E0)^2,
      f(X_T) >= [||E0||_* - sqrt(L) k Gamma_T]_+^2 / (2r).
    These are necessary loss obstructions, independent of momentum. They
    are not upper convergence guarantees or measured training times.
    Stronger bounds retain the whole spectrum and simultaneous rank,
    operator, and nuclear budgets. Hybrid bounds separate dense corrections.

  * A unified common-Frobenius stationarity bound retains each family's
    support function, update radius, oracle-input error, and approximation
    deficit. The supports are S_k(G), a S_k(G)+b||G||_F, and
    a S_k(G)+b eta||G||_1,entry, respectively.

  * Under the trajectory condition ||G||_* >= theta sqrt(r)||G||_F,
    exact-gradient/exact-oracle plain Fanions admit
      average ||G_t||_F <= sqrt(r/(theta^2 k)) sqrt(2 L Delta/T)
    at the stated horizon-tuned constant step. Thus rank changes the
    finite-time constant even though the deterministic exponent is shared.
    The stochastic tracking result also retains rank-dependent factors.

  * Exact flat-spectrum trajectories exhibit the kT time scale. A
    convexified continuous model has extinction time
    max(initial operator norm, initial nuclear norm/k), distinguishing
    low-rank speedup from its eventual saturation. The continuous model
    averages tied modes and is not a literal discrete rank-k trajectory.

  * Hybrid rank profiles can be nonmonotone. Explicit gradients with the
    same operator, Frobenius, and nuclear norms give opposite Fanion-5
    versus Muon comparisons. Three norm logs do not identify all ranks.

3. Evidence from the repository, and configuration limitations
-------------------------------------------------------------
The saved quadratic file code/long_run contains all 17 compared methods.
Without a fitted scale, Fanion-2 and Fanion-10 closely match Neon under
t -> k*t. Maximum discrepancies over their shared recorded horizons are
0.000252% and 0.01884% of initial loss, respectively.

Current source assigns F-Fanion-10 an inverse-square-root learning-rate
schedule, versus constant rates for the other methods. Over 5,000 updates
its cumulative rate is 2.09952 rather than 75. The saved CSV does not carry
an immutable configuration, so this is a source/trace audit, not a recovered
run manifest. Intermediate ranks also inherit endpoint hyperparameters.

The manuscript separates normalized CIFAR accuracy runs from unnormalized
gradient-log runs. A commented Neon call in the current script has a
normalization flag inconsistent with the table's description; the original
run configuration is needed. A reference norm CSV has nonfinite losses in
242 of 250 rows, while all gradient norms used are finite. No loss-rate
fit uses those entries. Original optimizer/gradient matrices are unavailable.

4. CIFAR and LLM theoretical description
---------------------------------------
CIFAR: use a batch-dependent, label-smoothed cross-entropy objective,
normalized convolution-weight geometry, tangent updates, and auxiliary SGD.
The code normalizes BEFORE subtracting an update. Its normalized states
admit an exact sphere-retraction identity with a radial-dependent step.
For ideal Fanion-k, the nominal relative Frobenius step is gamma sqrt(k/m).
At the inherited rates, ideal Neon is 8-16 times smaller than ideal Muon
on the six convolution matrices, before tangent projection. Rank-specific
tuning is needed to separate direction quality from update scale.

The actual 0.2 smoothing gives target entropy 0.86689391 and a sharp
misclassification cost of 0.48773025 above it. For a common prediction
mechanism and distribution,
  classification error <= (mean smoothed loss - entropy)/0.48773025.
Validation accuracy additionally requires generalization and control of
the evaluation-state change, including frozen BatchNorm statistics.

LLMs: use additive, shape-scaled matrix updates plus auxiliary Adam,
explicit component/decay scaling, and the code's tensor normalization groups.
The changing attention window makes the natural training objective F_t
time-dependent. A finite-horizon bound retains objective drift, dependent
token noise/bias, momentum, and the actual joint step's directional curvature.
The current LLM results mainly compare Muon with full-rank F/S variants;
they do not establish an all-rank Neon/Fanion LLM ranking.

5. Strongest candidate explanation for Muon's advantage
------------------------------------------------------
Many modes may require comparable corrections after curvature is accounted
for, even when their gradient singular values differ substantially. Small
gradients can reflect weak curvature. Truncating such modes can omit useful
corrections; approximate polar updates address many of them together.

In a positive local quadratic with modal gradient g and curvature K,
the condition g=e K 1 makes the full polar direction Newton-aligned.
Every positive-alignment direction d then retains the fraction
  (1^T K d)^2 / [(1^T K 1)(d^T K d)]
of the full direction's best scalar-step decrease. This handles every
Fanion rank, hybrid components (including their cross terms), and finite
spectral filters. For diagonal K and equal errors, the plain rank-k
fraction is selected curvature mass divided by total curvature mass.

An exactly embedded matrix example favors Muon over Neon by 6.82127 with
actual curvature, while a Frobenius surrogate gives 0.363514 and favors
Neon. This is an illustrative exact calculation, not a neural-data fit.
Whether this broad-correction condition holds for CIFAR/LLMs is unresolved.
If weak modes are noise or only a few corrections remain useful, a
truncated method can be preferable. Upper-bound comparisons alone also
do not prove an ordering of actual training times or validation accuracy.

Next discriminating measurements
-------------------------------
At a few checkpoints, record actual momentum inputs and displacements;
estimate independent-probe gradient alignment and joint directional
curvature; retain full/leading spectra and polynomial gains; compare
inherited, independently tuned, and tangent-size-matched rank schedules.
Keep normalized and unnormalized CIFAR runs separate and record context
rules for LLMs. These measurements would test the structural hypothesis.
