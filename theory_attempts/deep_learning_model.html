Describing CIFAR and LLM training theoretically:
geometry, curvature, and useful Fanion modes

Third research attempt for the Neon repository

21 October 2026

Abstract

The most promising hypothesis for the neural experiments concerns the curvature-adjusted correction, rather than a flat raw gradient spectrum. If many singular directions require comparable Newton corrections, Muon can address them together while Fanion-kk drops part of the useful correction. A precise local model proves this for every rank and describes the hybrid families through their angle in the curvature metric. It can favor Muon even when a Frobenius curvature surrogate favors Neon. For the actual experiments, CIFAR additionally requires normalized-weight geometry, batch-dependent losses, and label smoothing; the LLM speedrun requires shape-dependent steps, mixed optimizers, tensor batching, finite spectral filters, and an evolving attention window. We give a finite-horizon descent theorem accommodating these features, a sharp smoothed-loss bound on classification error, and measurements that could test the hypothesis. The local model is proved and numerically checked; its neural-network assumptions have not been established by the saved norm logs.

Recommended description and experimental scope

Describe the experiments as stochastic, coupled optimization over matrix blocks and auxiliary parameters, at a finite computational budget. The neural objective is not a sum of independent matrix quadratics. Layer-wise LMOs specify an update rule; they do not imply a separable loss. For CIFAR the appropriate geometric idealization is a product of weight spheres, with further scale redundancies from BatchNorm. For the LLMs the actual hidden-weight updates are additive; normalization of activations does not impose a sphere constraint on every weight matrix.

The comparison being explained.

The manuscript’s eight-epoch CIFAR table compares Muon, Neon, Fanion-5, their F variants, S-Muon, NSGD, and SignSGD. Its LLM speedrun results mainly establish that Muon, F-Muon, and S-Muon are close; they do not supply an all-rank Neon/Fanion LLM experiment. An explanation of the latter ranking would therefore be a prediction for a future experiment. Classification accuracy and held-out token cross-entropy are also different targets.

An objective in prediction space

Write the parameters as θ=(W1,…,WL,z)\theta=(W_1,\ldots,W_L,z), where zz contains parameters updated by SGD or Adam. For CIFAR the training objective is

FC(θ)=𝔼ℬ,a[1|ℬ|∑i∈ℬℓqi(sθ(xi;ℬ,a))],qi=0.8eyi+0.02𝟏.\label{eq:cifar-objective} F_{\mathrm C}(\theta)=\mathbb E_{\mathcal B,a}\left[ \frac1{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal B} \ell_{q_i}\big(s_\theta(x_i;\mathcal B,a)\big)\right], \qquad q_i=0.8e_{y_i}+0.02\boldsymbol 1.(1)
Here ss is the final logit vector and aa denotes augmentation. BatchNorm makes a prediction depend on the other examples in the batch, so this is an expectation of batch losses, not automatically the average of fixed per-example functions. Frozen running statistics define the evaluation predictor separately. Random reshuffling also does not give the usual conditionally unbiased IID gradient at every step.

For a language model with attention context rule cc, write

Fc(θ)=𝔼document token position[−logpθ(xj∣x<j;c)].\label{eq:llm-objective} F_c(\theta)=\mathbb E_{\text{document token position}} \left[-\log p_\theta(x_j\mid x_{<j};c)\right].(2)
Use mean per-token cross-entropy for theoretical comparisons. A fixed batch-size factor in summed gradients cancels in ideal homogeneous matrix oracles, but must be restored when specifying noise variance, epsilon effects, auxiliary optimizers, or an actual parameter displacement. Document dependence prevents interpreting every token as an independent gradient sample. For the speedrun c=ctc=c_t changes, so the natural training objective is Ft=FctF_t=F_{c_t}. One can analyze fixed-window phases, or retain the objective drift explicitly. A theorem for a fixed full-context objective would instead have to account for the shorter-context gradient bias.

For fixed samples and context, let JtJ_t be the Jacobian of final logits, rt=∇sℓr_t=\nabla_s\ell, and Ct=∇s2ℓC_t=\nabla_s^2\ell. For cross-entropy, Ct=diag⁡(p)−pp⊤≽0C_t=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\succeq0 per prediction. The Gauss–Newton form of the joint curvature is

Gt=Jt⊤rt,KtGN=Jt⊤CtJt,⟨V,KtGNV⟩=⟨JtV,CtJtV⟩.\label{eq:ggn} G_t=J_t^\top r_t,\qquad K_t^{\mathrm{GN}}=J_t^\top C_tJ_t,\qquad \langle V,K_t^{\mathrm{GN}}V\rangle=\langle J_tV,C_tJ_tV\rangle.(3)
Sample/token averaging is implicit. JtVJ_tV includes all matrix and auxiliary updates and their interactions. For a nonlinear model the true Hessian additionally contains ∑j(rt)j∇θ2(sθ)j\sum_j (r_t)_j\nabla_\theta^2(s_\theta)_j. A rigorous upper curvature bound must control that residual and curvature changes along the step. At nonsmooth pooling/ReLU boundaries a local upper-model inequality can replace Hessian notation.

The Kronecker form ⟨Vl,AlVlBl⟩\langle V_l,A_lV_lB_l\rangle is a useful layer approximation to a Fisher or Gauss–Newton block; see Martens and Grosse (2015). It is not automatically a bound on the full neural Hessian. Cross-layer terms, correlations in convolutional patches, and BatchNorm coupling require estimates or a stated domination assumption. In particular, declaring both factors isotropic discards precisely the curvature structure that may explain Muon’s advantage.

CIFAR: the exact normalization geometry

For a matrix of output width mlm_l, set Rl=mlR_l=\sqrt{m_l} and Nl(W)=RlW/‖W‖FN_l(W)=R_lW/\lVert W\rVert_{\mathrm F}. The current normalized implementations perform

Hl,t=momentum from gradients at Wt,Wl,t+1=Nl(Wl,t)−γl,tDl,t(Hl,t).\label{eq:before} H_{l,t}=\text{momentum from gradients at }W_t,\qquad W_{l,t+1}=N_l(W_{l,t})-\gamma_{l,t}D_{l,t}(H_{l,t}).(4)
This is not literally post-update projection. Nevertheless its normalized states Yl,t=Nl(Wl,t)Y_{l,t}=N_l(W_{l,t}) satisfy an exact retraction formula.

Proposition 1 (Normalization before a step). For ‖Y‖F=R\lVert Y\rVert_{\mathrm F}=R, decompose D=D⊤+αYD=D_\top+\alpha Y, where α=⟨Y,D⟩/R2\alpha=\langle Y,D\rangle/R^2 and ⟨Y,D⊤⟩=0\langle Y,D_\top\rangle=0. If 1−γα>01-\gamma\alpha>0,

N(Y−γD)=N(Y−ηD⊤),η=γ1−γα.\label{eq:retraction} N(Y-\gamma D)=N(Y-\eta D_\top),\qquad \eta=\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma\alpha}.(5)
Thus (4), viewed on normalized states, is a tangent step with a radial-dependent effective learning rate followed by a sphere retraction.

Proof. Factor Y−γD=(1−γα)(Y−ηD⊤)Y-\gamma D=(1-\gamma\alpha)(Y-\eta D_\top) and use the invariance of NN under positive scalar multiplication. A sufficient condition for the denominator is γ‖D‖F<R\gamma\lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm F}<R. ◻

For zero BatchNorm epsilon, positive scaling of each of these convolution weights leaves training predictions unchanged: convolution and max pooling scale together and BatchNorm cancels that scale before GELU. Per-channel positive scalings give additional redundancies. At the sphere representative, the relevant gradient is

Gltan=Gl−⟨Gl,Yl⟩Rl2Yl,⟨Gltan,Dltan⟩=⟨Gltan,Dl⟩.\label{eq:tangent} G_l^{\mathrm{tan}}=G_l-\frac{\langle G_l,Y_l\rangle}{R_l^2}Y_l,\qquad \langle G_l^{\mathrm{tan}},D_l^{\mathrm{tan}}\rangle =\langle G_l^{\mathrm{tan}},D_l\rangle.(6)
For an exactly scale-invariant training loss GlG_l is already tangent. If Wl=clYlW_l=c_lY_l, then ∇YlF(Y,z)=cl∇WlF(W,z)\nabla_{Y_l}F(Y,z)=c_l\nabla_{W_l}F(W,z). The momentum still contains gradients from previous, differently scaled states; it must be retained as actually computed rather than identified with the current tangent gradient.

Real epsilon, finite precision, and frozen evaluation statistics prevent treating all these statements as exact properties of the implemented evaluation predictor. They justify a clear idealization, with a residual term if needed. Without scale invariance one must directly retain F(N(W))−F(W)F(N(W))-F(W) in an ambient-space descent argument, or study the actual two-stage map. One cannot silently replace it by projected gradient descent.

A concrete rank-dependent scale.

For the ideal plain Fanion direction ‖Dk‖F=k\lVert D_k\rVert_{\mathrm F}=\sqrt{k} and all six convolutions have rl=mlr_l=m_l. Before tangent projection its relative step is

γ‖Dk‖FRl=γk/ml.\label{eq:rank-step} \frac{\gamma\lVert D_k\rVert_{\mathrm F}}{R_l}=\gamma\sqrt{k/m_l}.(7)
At the inherited learning rate 0.240.24, the scales are:

Output width Neon Fanion-5 Muon Frob.-matched Neon lr
64 0.030 0.0671 0.240 1.92
256 0.015 0.0335 0.240 3.84

These are direction-norm scales, not measured angles: projection, radial components, and the polynomial filter can change them. They show why inherited learning rates do not establish the best attainable performance of each rank. A useful ablation separately tunes every rank, includes γl,k=γl,rrl/k\gamma_{l,k}=\gamma_{l,r}\sqrt{r_l/k} as one starting rule, and measures the actual tangent step. Matching Frobenius size still does not match curvature, spectral norm, or prediction change; those are distinct controls.

The CIFAR mixture rates partially compensate for the half-sized polar component: 0.4/2=0.200.4/2=0.20 for F-Muon and 0.42/2=0.210.42/2=0.21 for S-Muon, versus 0.240.24 for Muon. For a=ba=b, the extra F component has Frobenius size 1/rl1/\sqrt{r_l} relative to the full polar component, or 6.25–12.5% here. For S-Muon with η=0.003\eta=0.003, a dense sign component has ratio ηmlnl/rl=ηnl\eta\sqrt{m_ln_l/r_l}=\eta\sqrt{n_l}, or 4.4–14.4%. These small perturbations make similar behavior plausible after tuning. Curvature can amplify them, so Frobenius closeness alone is insufficient.

The earlier additive quadratic rank and nuclear-budget bounds do not transfer unchanged through normalization: scaling the current full weight adds a radial full-rank displacement. On normalized states that direction is a scale redundancy, not an ordinary rank-kk correction to a fixed target.

LLMs: coupled blocks and finite spectral filters

Using a common schedule factor γt\gamma_t, represent the actual additive step as

θt+1=θt−γtVt,(Vt)l=ωlalD̃kl(Hl,t)+ω̃lblZl,t+λlWl,t.\label{eq:llm-step} \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\gamma_t V_t,\qquad (V_t)_l=\omega_l a_l\widetilde D_{k_l}(H_{l,t}) +\widetilde\omega_l b_l Z_{l,t}+\lambda_lW_{l,t}.(8)
Here Z=H/‖H‖FZ=H/\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm F} for F, and Z=ηsign⁡HZ=\eta\operatorname{sign}H for S; scaling can instead be absorbed into ZZ. ωl\omega_l includes shape/parameter multipliers; ω̃l\widetilde\omega_l records whether the dense part is scaled. The auxiliary component of VtV_t is the actual Adam displacement divided by γt\gamma_t. Decoupled decay belongs in VtV_t; for a general matrix oracle it is not obtained by simply replacing GG by G+λWG+\lambda W inside the LMO.

For a QKV tuple H=(H1,H2,H3)H=(H_1,H_2,H_3), the NanoGPT plain spectral support is a sum across slices, while the F component is (H1,H2,H3)/(∑j‖Hj‖F2)1/2(H_1,H_2,H_3)/(\sum_j\lVert H_j\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2)^{1/2}. This differs from three separately unit-normalized dense components. Keep the code’s normalization group as part of the theoretical block definition. The paper’s textual sign coefficient and a script’s sign_lr_mult should be translated into the actual two component displacements; they need not denote the same η\eta in (8).

The implemented Muon is a spectral filter

Let H=Udiag⁡(si)V⊤H=U\operatorname{diag}(s_i)V^\top. Ignoring epsilon and rounding, a finite polynomial iteration gives

D̃r(H)=Udiag⁡(wi)V⊤,wi=(pq∘⋯∘p1)(si/‖H‖F),pj(x)=ajx+bjx3+cjx5.\label{eq:filter} \widetilde D_r(H)=U\operatorname{diag}(w_i)V^\top,\quad w_i=(p_q\circ\cdots\circ p_1)(s_i/\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm F}),\quad p_j(x)=a_jx+b_jx^3+c_jx^5.(9)
This follows by substituting the SVD in each matrix iteration; it is also the scalar-filter description in Jordan’s original writeup. For CIFAR q=3q=3 and NanoGPT q=5q=5, with repeated coefficients (3.4445,−4.7750,2.0315)(3.4445,-4.7750,2.0315). Near zero their gains are respectively 40.87x40.87x and 484.88x484.88x; zero modes remain zero. The Medium script instead uses five different polynomials, whose composition must be retained. Modes are neither uniformly set to one nor guaranteed to stay below one. Consequently an ideal feasible-polar LMO theorem needs an explicit approximation/feasibility correction, or can be replaced by the actual-direction theorem below.

This supplies a useful middle model between hard truncation and an ideal polar update: preserve every direction with its actual gain wiw_i. It accommodates every Fanion rank, either with hard indicators or measured approximate gains, and the extra dense components. Whether weak modes carry signal or noise must be measured independently.

Momentum, dependence, and gradient alignment

The CIFAR buffer is Bt=βBt−1+gtB_t=\beta B_{t-1}+g_t, with Ht=gt+βBtH_t=g_t+\beta B_t. The LLM buffer is Mt=βMt−1+(1−β)gtM_t=\beta M_{t-1}+(1-\beta)g_t, with Ht=(1−β)gt+βMtH_t=(1-\beta)g_t+\beta M_t. For constant β\beta and zero initialization, Mt=(1−β)BtM_t=(1-\beta)B_t, so the two oracle inputs differ only by the positive factor 1−β1-\beta. Ideal homogeneous update maps coincide. This equivalence need not hold when momentum varies.

For the LLM’s deterministic warmup schedule, the normalized input has weights

Ht=∑s=0twt,sgs,wt,t=1−βt2,wt,s=βt(1−βs)∏j=s+1tβj(s<t),∑swt,s=1−βt∏j=0tβj.\label{eq:momentum-weights} H_t=\sum_{s=0}^t w_{t,s}g_s,\quad w_{t,t}=1-\beta_t^2,\quad w_{t,s}=\beta_t(1-\beta_s)\prod_{j=s+1}^t\beta_j\ (s<t),\quad \sum_s w_{t,s}=1-\beta_t\prod_{j=0}^t\beta_j.(10)
For constant momentum these reduce to the weights analyzed in fanion_followup.pdf. If gs=Gs+bs+ξsg_s=G_s+b_s+\xi_s, with deterministic weights and martingale differences ξs\xi_s of second moment at most σs2\sigma_s^2, then
𝔼‖Ht−Gt‖F≤mt𝔼‖Gt‖F+∑swt,s𝔼‖Gs−Gt‖F+∑swt,s𝔼‖bs‖F+(∑swt,s2σs2)1/2,mt=1−∑swt,s.\label{eq:tracking} \mathbb E\lVert H_t-G_t\rVert_{\mathrm F}\leq m_t\mathbb E\lVert G_t\rVert_{\mathrm F}+\sum_s w_{t,s}\mathbb E\lVert G_s-G_t\rVert_{\mathrm F} +\sum_s w_{t,s}\mathbb E\lVert b_s\rVert_{\mathrm F} +\left(\sum_s w_{t,s}^2\sigma_s^2\right)^{1/2}, \quad m_t=1-\sum_s w_{t,s}.(11)
The proof is the triangle inequality and orthogonality of martingale increments. Changes in parameters and context rule enter Gs−GtG_s-G_t. Bias bsb_s allows reshuffling, document-stream dependence, or an objective mismatch. This is a conditional noise description, not a claim that token gradients are IID or that variance divides by token count.

Most importantly, the useful alignment is ⟨∇Ft,Dk(Ht)⟩\langle \nabla F_t,D_k(H_t)\rangle, not the always-positive self-alignment ⟨Ht,Dk(Ht)⟩\langle H_t,D_k(H_t)\rangle. Finite-batch nonlinear transformations may be biased. Do, Dereich, and Jentzen (2026) give relevant stochastic counterexamples and batch-size-dependent error analysis for generalized Muon. This does not show failure on these neural tasks; it rules out treating fixed-batch consistency as automatic.

A finite-horizon theorem for both settings

Let xtx_t be the additive LLM state, or the sphere representatives and auxiliary parameters for the scale-invariant CIFAR idealization. Write the step as xt+1=ℛxt(−γtVt)x_{t+1}=\mathcal R_{x_t}(-\gamma_t V_t), using the identity map for additive updates and a product retraction for CIFAR. In the latter case the llth component of VtV_t is (ηl,t/γt)Dl,ttan(\eta_{l,t}/\gamma_t)D_{l,t}^{\mathrm{tan}} from (5). Retain auxiliary updates in the same vector.

Theorem 2 (Actual-direction descent with objective drift). Suppose along the executed steps that

Ft(ℛxt(−γtVt))≤Ft(xt)−γtAt+12γt2Bt,At=⟨grad⁡Ft(xt),Vt⟩,Bt≥0.\label{eq:local-upper} F_t(\mathcal R_{x_t}(-\gamma_t V_t)) \leq F_t(x_t)-\gamma_t A_t+\tfrac12\gamma_t^2 B_t, \qquad A_t=\langle \operatorname{grad}F_t(x_t),V_t\rangle,\quad B_t\geq0.(12)
Define δt=Ft+1(xt+1)−Ft(xt+1)\delta_t=F_{t+1}(x_{t+1})-F_t(x_{t+1}) and assume FT(xT)≥Fmin⁡F_T(x_T)\geq F_{\min}. For every realized trajectory,
∑t<TγtAt≤F0(x0)−Fmin⁡+12∑t<Tγt2Bt+∑t<Tδt.\label{eq:telescope} \sum_{t<T}\gamma_t A_t \leq F_0(x_0)-F_{\min} +\tfrac12\sum_{t<T}\gamma_t^2 B_t +\sum_{t<T}\delta_t.(13)
Replacing the last sum by ∑t[δt]+\sum_t[\delta_t]_+ gives a conservative bound. Taking expectations preserves the inequality when the terms are integrable.

Proof. Add δt\delta_t to (12) and sum; the objective values telescope. No independence or convexity is used. For a fixed context all δt\delta_t vanish. An additive upper-model error can be added to the right-hand side and telescopes in the same way. ◻

The theorem exposes the quantities a rate comparison must retain: alignment with the target gradient, curvature of the actual joint step, and context/objective drift. Substituting a global Frobenius smoothness bound is valid when available but can destroy the comparison. For example, a structured certificate could bound BtB_t by χt[∑l⟨Vl,AlVlBl⟩+Cz(Vz)]\chi_t[\sum_l\langle V_l,A_lV_lB_l\rangle+C_z(V_z)] after proving a cross-block and nonlinear-residual bound. A raw Hessian-vector product at one point is an estimate, not itself a certificate over a finite segment.

Optional conditional linear rate.

In a fixed-objective phase, suppose At>0A_t>0, Bt>0B_t>0 and a local Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition holds above the target loss: ‖gradF‖F2≥2μ(F−F*)\lVert \operatorname{grad}F\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2\geq2\mu(F-F_*). Define the direction quality qt=At2/(Bt‖gradF‖F2)q_t=A_t^2/(B_t\lVert \operatorname{grad}F\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2) and schedule ratio ρt=γtBt/At\rho_t=\gamma_tB_t/A_t. If ρt∈(0,2)\rho_t\in(0,2), (12) gives

F(xt+1)−F*≤[1−μqtρt(2−ρt)](F(xt)−F*).\label{eq:conditional-rate} F(x_{t+1})-F_* \leq[1-\mu q_t\rho_t(2-\rho_t)](F(x_t)-F_*).(14)
This follows by writing the decrease as At2ρt(2−ρt)/(2Bt)A_t^2\rho_t(2-\rho_t)/(2B_t). It identifies both a direction factor and a tuning factor. Neither a global PL condition nor uniformly positive alignment has been established for these training runs. In a noisy regime use an expected version with explicit alignment/error assumptions; do not infer this deterministic rate from same-batch norms.

A more useful hypothesis: comparable Newton corrections

The key question is which modes need changing in prediction space after curvature is accounted for. A small gradient singular value can reflect small curvature, rather than a completed or useless mode. This suggests testing broad Newton corrections instead of assuming a broad raw gradient spectrum.

At a fixed point let Pi=uivi⊤P_i=u_iv_i^\top be the singular directions of the actual oracle input. For CIFAR replace these by their tangent projections and use the retraction pullback. Define

gi=⟨grad⁡F,Pi⟩,Kij=⟨Pi,ℋPj⟩,𝒬(d)=(g⊤d)2d⊤Kd(g⊤d>0).\label{eq:modal-model} g_i=\langle \operatorname{grad}F,P_i\rangle,\qquad K_{ij}=\langle P_i,\mathcal H P_j\rangle,\qquad \mathcal Q(d)=\frac{(g^\top d)^2}{d^\top Kd}\quad(g^\top d>0).(15)
Here ℋ\mathcal H is an exact positive quadratic curvature or a valid local upper model. For an exact quadratic, 𝒬(d)/2\mathcal Q(d)/2 is the loss decrease under the best scalar step in direction ∑idiPi\sum_i d_iP_i. The rank-kk plain direction has di=𝟏i≤kd_i=\boldsymbol1_{i\leq k}. Finite Muon uses the gains in (9); F adds bsi/‖H‖Fb s_i/\lVert H\rVert_{\mathrm F} to its spectral weights. For S, retain sign⁡H\operatorname{sign}H as an extra direction and extend g,Kg,K with its curvature and cross terms. Its component outside the span of the PiP_i must not be dropped.

Proposition 3 (Newton-aligned full polar direction). Suppose K≻0K\succ0 on these coordinates and

g=eK𝟏,e>0.\label{eq:newton-alignment} g=eK\boldsymbol1,\qquad e>0.(16)
Then the full ideal Muon direction is the optimal direction in this local quadratic model, and for every Fanion or hybrid coefficient vector dd,
𝒬(d)𝒬(𝟏)=(𝟏⊤Kd)2(𝟏⊤K𝟏)(d⊤Kd)=cos⁡K2(𝟏,d)≤1.\label{eq:efficiency} \frac{\mathcal Q(d)}{\mathcal Q(\boldsymbol1)} =\frac{(\boldsymbol1^\top Kd)^2} {(\boldsymbol1^\top K\boldsymbol1)(d^\top Kd)} =\cos_K^2(\boldsymbol1,d)\leq1.(17)
Only directions of positive alignment are used. If the actual Newton correction has zero coordinates outside the polar span, the same statement holds after adding other directions, including the sign direction.

Proof. The minimizer of the quadratic model has correction K−1g=e𝟏K^{-1}g=e\boldsymbol1. Substitution gives (17); its upper bound is Cauchy–Schwarz in the KK inner product. The optimal scalar step for the full polar direction is ee. ◻

This is a sufficient, testable structural hypothesis, not a conclusion deduced from the choice of optimizer. It can also be used with the implemented full-filter direction ww: the corresponding hypothesis is g=eKwg=eKw. The local theorem is then exact for that direction. Replacing an indefinite Hessian by a PSD Gauss–Newton or trust-region upper model changes the hypothesis and must be stated.

Robustness of the hypothesis.

If g=eK𝟏+hg=eK\boldsymbol1+h and ‖K−1/2h‖F≤δe‖K1/2𝟏‖F\lVert K^{-1/2}h\rVert_{\mathrm F}\leq\delta e\lVert K^{1/2}\boldsymbol 1\rVert_{\mathrm F}, 0≤δ<10\leq\delta<1, then the full polar score relative to the best possible local score obeys

𝒬(𝟏)g⊤K−1g≥(1−δ1+δ)2.\label{eq:robust} \frac{\mathcal Q(\boldsymbol1)}{g^\top K^{-1}g} \geq\left(\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\right)^2.(18)
Indeed its numerator’s alignment is at least e(1−δ)𝟏⊤K𝟏e(1-\delta)\boldsymbol1^\top K\boldsymbol1, while ‖K−1/2g‖F≤e(1+δ)‖K1/2𝟏‖F\lVert K^{-1/2}g\rVert_{\mathrm F}\leq e(1+\delta)\lVert K^{1/2}\boldsymbol 1\rVert_{\mathrm F}. Likewise, under exact (16), a hybrid d=a𝟏+bzd=a\boldsymbol1+bz with ‖K1/2bz‖F≤εa‖K1/2𝟏‖F\lVert K^{1/2}bz\rVert_{\mathrm F}\leq\varepsilon a\lVert K^{1/2}\boldsymbol 1\rVert_{\mathrm F}, a>0a>0, ε<1\varepsilon<1, retains at least ((1−ε)/(1+ε))2((1-\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon))^2 of the full score. This allows a negative bb as in the manuscript’s α>1\alpha>1 experiments, provided the perturbation bound holds; a convex-LMO-sum argument would not cover that case.

Every Fanion rank in a solvable curvature model

Suppose the modal curvature is diagonal with entries λi>0\lambda_i>0 and the current modal errors are ei>0e_i>0. Then gi=λieig_i=\lambda_i e_i. When ei=ee_i=e for all ii, for the set IkI_k selected from the oracle input,

𝒬k=e2∑i∈Ikλi,𝒬r𝒬k=∑i≤rλi∑i∈Ikλi.\label{eq:curvature-mass} \mathcal Q_k=e^2\sum_{i\in I_k}\lambda_i,\qquad \frac{\mathcal Q_r}{\mathcal Q_k} =\frac{\sum_{i\leq r}\lambda_i}{\sum_{i\in I_k}\lambda_i}.(19)
For an exact-gradient input H=GH=G, IkI_k comprises the kk largest λi\lambda_i. For a general momentum input the denominator is at most that largest-kk curvature mass, giving a lower bound on the score ratio. The effective rank governing the full-versus-top-mode comparison is the curvature mass ∑iλi/λ1\sum_i\lambda_i/\lambda_1 in the exact-gradient case, rather than (∑iλi)2/(rλ12)(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2/(r\lambda_1^2) from a Frobenius surrogate. For 0<emin⁡≤ei≤emax⁡0<e_{\min}\leq e_i\leq e_{\max}, even if sorting by gradient and sorting by curvature differ,
𝒬r𝒬k≥(emin⁡emax⁡)2∑iλi∑i≤kλi↓.\label{eq:comparable-errors} \frac{\mathcal Q_r}{\mathcal Q_k} \geq\left(\frac{e_{\min}}{e_{\max}}\right)^2 \frac{\sum_i\lambda_i}{\sum_{i\leq k}\lambda_i^{\downarrow}}.(20)
For the numerator use (∑iλiei)2/∑iλi≥emin⁡2∑iλi(\sum_i\lambda_i e_i)^2/\sum_i\lambda_i \geq e_{\min}^2\sum_i\lambda_i; for the selected denominator use 𝒬k≤emax⁡2∑i∈Ikλi≤emax⁡2∑i≤kλi↓\mathcal Q_k\leq e_{\max}^2\sum_{i\in I_k}\lambda_i \leq e_{\max}^2\sum_{i\leq k}\lambda_i^{\downarrow}.

For any positive-alignment spectral filter or hybrid weights did_i and equal errors, the fraction of the optimal decrease is

𝒬(d)e2∑iλi=(𝔼πd)2𝔼πd2=11+Var⁡π(d)/(𝔼πd)2,πi=λi∑jλj.\label{eq:cv} \frac{\mathcal Q(d)}{e^2\sum_i\lambda_i} =\frac{(\mathbb E_\pi d)^2}{\mathbb E_\pi d^2} =\frac{1}{1+\operatorname{Var}_\pi(d)/(\mathbb E_\pi d)^2}, \qquad \pi_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_j\lambda_j}.(21)
Thus Muon’s near-constant weights can be good in the curvature metric, F-Muon’s weights can be a modest perturbation, and truncated Fanions lose useful curvature mass. A sign update may have extra off-modal curvature; its behavior additionally depends on the entry basis. These are explicit mechanisms for closeness of the full-rank hybrids and weakness of small ranks, with no universal ordering when the structural hypothesis fails.

What convergence target matches CIFAR accuracy?

For CC classes and smoothing ε∈[0,1)\varepsilon\in[0,1), let qh=1−ε+ε/Cq_h=1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon/C and ql=ε/Cq_l=\varepsilon/C. For an example with correct label yy,

ℓq(p)=H(q)+KL⁡(q‖p),κε,C=qhlog⁡2qhqh+ql+qllog⁡2qlqh+ql.\label{eq:smoothed-gap} \ell_q(p)=H(q)+\operatorname{KL}(q\Vert p),\qquad \kappa_{\varepsilon,C} =q_h\log\frac{2q_h}{q_h+q_l} +q_l\log\frac{2q_l}{q_h+q_l}.(23)
Use the convention 0log⁡0=00\log0=0.

Proposition 4 (A sharp mistake cost for smoothed cross-entropy). If a class other than yy has probability at least pyp_y, then ℓq(p)−H(q)≥κε,C\ell_q(p)-H(q)\geq\kappa_{\varepsilon,C}. Consequently, for the same prediction mechanism and distribution,

Pr⁡(ŷ≠y)≤𝔼ℓq(p)−H(q)κε,C.\label{eq:error-bound} \Pr(\widehat y\ne y) \leq\frac{\mathbb E\ell_q(p)-H(q)}{\kappa_{\varepsilon,C}}.(24)

Proof. For a fixed competing class jj, minimize KL⁡(q‖p)\operatorname{KL}(q\Vert p) subject to pj≥pyp_j\geq p_y. The optimum pools the two coordinates: py=pj=(qh+ql)/2p_y=p_j=(q_h+q_l)/2, with pi=qip_i=q_i otherwise. This follows from the Lagrange conditions, or convexity of −∑iqilog⁡pi-\sum_iq_i\log p_i. The resulting KL is (23), independent of jj. Take the union over possible wrong maximizers pointwise (each has the same minimum cost), then average the indicator inequality. ◻

For the actual CIFAR smoothing ε=0.2\varepsilon=0.2, C=10C=10,

H(q)=0.86689391,κ0.2,10=0.48773025,log⁡(qh/ql)=log⁡41=3.71357207.\label{eq:cifar-constants} H(q)=0.86689391,\qquad \kappa_{0.2,10}=0.48773025, \qquad \log(q_h/q_l)=\log41=3.71357207.(25)
Units are natural-log cross-entropy. Perfect training classification does not require vanishing gradients or attaining H(q)H(q): it only requires the correct class to have the largest probability. Moreover the smoothed logit optimum has a finite target margin log⁡41\log41; a zero-loss or unbounded-margin model for unsmoothed separable classification would misdescribe this experiment.

The inequality links training optimization to training error, or population loss to population error. For validation accuracy, one additionally needs the appropriate loss generalization control and a bound on changing the prediction mechanism (augmentation, frozen BatchNorm statistics, and test augmentation). A training-mode gradient theorem alone does not rank the reported validation accuracies. Even (24) is an upper bound, so comparing its values cannot prove a strict actual-accuracy ordering.

For LLM evaluation, the target is already held-out mean token cross-entropy. There is no need to replace it by classification accuracy or by a vanishing parameter-gradient target. Population versus finite held-out loss still requires a sampling statement if a statistical claim is intended. The bounded logit transformation in the NanoGPT script further means that the usual unrestricted softmax infinite-margin limit is not its literal model.

Measurements that would decide between explanations

The new structural assumption is not checkable from Frobenius, operator, and nuclear norms alone. The following measurements at a few shared checkpoints would make the theory discriminating.

  1. Record the actual configuration and displacement. For every group save the normalization flag and order, matrix/tensor shape, learning-rate multipliers, the two hybrid component steps, polynomial coefficients, momentum convention, and auxiliary update. For CIFAR also save WW, N(W)N(W), the radial component, and the tangent displacement. For the LLM save context/window and summed-versus-mean gradient scaling.

  2. Use an independent probe gradient. At the same state and context, form the candidate directions from the same actual momentum input HH, then estimate ⟨∇Ft,V⟩\langle \nabla F_t,V\rangle on fresh training batches/documents. This diagnoses the update map without its same-batch self-alignment bias. BatchNorm state must be kept consistent during probes. At each algorithm’s own trajectory also log its own HH; a common-input counterfactual does not replace that.

  3. Measure curvature and actual loss change. Estimate V⊤KGNVV^\top K^{\mathrm{GN}}V by a joint Jacobian-vector product and compare with a Hessian-vector product or symmetric small loss probes. Include matrix/auxiliary and cross-layer interactions, and for CIFAR probe the actual retraction. Check finite-step Taylor error rather than treating a pointwise curvature as an upper bound. Both A2/BA^2/B and the actual scheduled decrease γA−γ2B/2\gamma A-\gamma^2B/2 matter.

  4. Resolve all ranks. Log leading singular values of HH beyond ranks 1 and 5, the polynomial gains, and gi=⟨Gprobe,Pi⟩g_i=\langle G_{\mathrm{probe}},P_i\rangle. Estimate the modal Gram matrix or selected diagonal/cross terms. The question is whether the inferred correction K−1gK^{-1}g is broad and approximately constant in the polar modes, and whether curvature mass outside the first kk directions is large. Do not infer this from a heavy-tailed gradient spectrum alone.

  5. Separate tuning and generalization. Compare inherited, independently tuned, and tangent/Frobenius-matched rank schedules. Keep normalized and unnormalized CIFAR runs separate. For an accuracy explanation, examine train loss, validation loss, and the normalization/evaluation gap alongside accuracy. For LLMs compare fixed context phases or report the context drift.

These measurements yield competing predictions. If the useful correction is broad but gradients are anisotropic, the curvature model predicts a Muon advantage and near-equivalence of small full-rank perturbations. If weak modes are mostly noise, their independent alignments will be small or negative and a truncated/soft-filter method can be better. If the gap largely vanishes after rank-dependent retuning, step scale explained much of the original inherited-hyperparameter gap. The results should be used to select a structural hypothesis, not to assert that one already holds.

What is established, and what is still a hypothesis

Established here are the exact normalization reindexing, finite-filter representation, variable-momentum weights, drift-aware descent theorem, local curvature/Newton comparison for every rank, the smoothed-loss classification bound, and an exactly embedded example reversing a Frobenius-based rank prediction. The analysis script passes 3,378 numerical checks and records input hashes and limitations in results/deep_learning/summary.json. Its examples are deterministic mathematical diagnostics, with random seed 20261021, not neural experiments.

For the article, the strongest defensible description is: “Muon can exploit a broad useful correction in the network’s local curvature geometry. Low-rank Fanions can miss a substantial fraction of that correction; full-rank F and S perturbations can retain most of it. The explanation depends on projected/functional alignment, directional curvature, and the effective schedule, which are not fixed by asymptotic exponents or by three raw gradient norms.” This remains a proposed mechanism for the CIFAR/LLM results until the stated quantities are measured. It does not establish a universal optimizer ordering, a global neural convergence rate, or a generalization theorem.
