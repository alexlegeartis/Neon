Fanions at every rank:
finite budgets, hybrid crossovers, and Nesterov tracking

Second research attempt for the Neon repository

21 October 2026

Abstract

We extend the comparison to every rank kk in the plain, F-, and S-Fanion families. A single convergence theorem retains their different support functions, gradient spectra, entry density, cumulative learning rates, and oracle errors. Its momentum corollary uses the repository’s actual Nesterov convention. We strengthen the quadratic lower bounds by retaining the whole initial singular spectrum and by separating a hybrid’s rank-limited and dense updates. An exactly solvable spectral flow explains both 1/k1/k progress and its saturation; hybrid examples identify different crossover ranks. The saved quadratic traces provide unusually close evidence for the low-rank time scale: Fanion-2 and Fanion-10 nearly coincide with Neon after rescaling time by kk. A source audit also finds a different learning-rate schedule for F-Fanion-10. The results provide a substantial explanation for the quadratic experiments. They do not yet prove the CIFAR/LLM accuracy ordering.

What changed in this attempt

The first note, bounds.pdf, established rank and nuclear-budget obstructions and a gradient/curvature score. This note develops the missing rank curve, including all intermediate Fanions and both hybrid families. All code and outputs are in theory_attempts; the submission and benchmark code have not been modified.

Three useful conclusions.

  1. At the same small learning rate, low-rank Fanions spend nuclear update budget at rate kγk\gamma. This predicts approximately kk times faster progress while that budget is the binding obstruction. It is exceptionally consistent with the saved rank-2 and rank-10 quadratic trajectories.

  2. F- and S-Fanions supply a dense update as well. Their relevant support functions are aSk+b‖G‖FaS_k+b\lVert G\rVert_{\mathrm F} and aSk+bη‖G‖1,entryaS_k+b\eta\lVert G\rVert_{1,\mathrm{entry}}, respectively. The extra terms explain why low-rank hybrids can behave very differently from plain Fanions, and why increasing their rank need not give the same kk-fold gain.

  3. A complete ordering needs spectral shape, curvature, and momentum alignment. Three gradient norms determine the endpoint comparison but do not determine the intermediate ranks, even in a noiseless isotropic quadratic. We give an explicit counterexample and useful intervals.

A concrete experimental confound.

In the current code/L_smooth_tests/run_benchmarks_new.py, F-Fanion-10 alone uses inv_sqrt_lr; its other Fanion specifications use const_lr. Over 5,000 updates, its cumulative learning rate is 2.09952 rather than 75. The slow F-Fanion-10 trace therefore cannot be used as a comparison of ranks at a common schedule. The CSV itself does not serialize configuration; this diagnosis uses the current source and its consistency with the saved trace, rather than claiming a recovered run manifest.

What the hybrid constants predict.

For F-Fanions, the support terms become comparable at k≈(b/a)r/θ0k\approx(b/a)\sqrt r/\theta_0. Below this scale, the normalized-gradient part can dominate the guaranteed projection even though it has a small coefficient. For S-Fanions the analogous scale is k≈(b/a)ηc0rd/θ0k\approx(b/a)\eta c_0\sqrt{rd}/\theta_0. The curvature radii also change with kk, so these projection crossovers are not an assertion that the optimally tuned rank curve must be monotone.

Implementation scope.

These results cover the ideal directions and feasible approximate oracles with a recorded support deficit. The repository’s finite Newton–Schulz polynomial need not satisfy ‖D‖op≤1\lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1 exactly. Its actual norm and projection error must be inserted before using this theorem for practical Muon. The tiny denominator regularizer in normalized-gradient code is also an oracle approximation. Theorems assume additive updates; weight normalization or retractions require a separate calculation.

Proof. Von Neumann’s trace inequality shows that the best correction aligns its singular vectors with those of E0E_0. The remaining scalar problem minimizes ∑i≤p(si−ai)2/2\sum_{i\leq p}(s_i-a_i)^2/2 subject to 0≤ai≤P0\leq a_i\leq P and ∑ai≤B\sum a_i\leq B. Its optimality conditions give the clipped threshold in (9). This lower bound simultaneously includes the earlier rank-tail and nuclear bounds. The operator budget matters at high kk: serving all modes at once cannot remove the largest singular error faster than the allowed per-mode step.

A whole-spectrum continuous model

For the isotropic quadratic, consider the convexified spectral flow Ẋ∈−∂Sk(X)\dot X\in-\partial S_k(X). At a tie, this allows averages of tied rank-kk maximizers. If X0=Udiag⁡(s)V⊤X_0=U\operatorname{diag}(s)V^\top, its solution is

X(u)=Udiag⁡(si−ai(u))V⊤,ai(u)=min⁡{u,(si−τ(u))+},\label{eq:flow} X(u)=U\operatorname{diag}(s_i-a_i(u))V^\top,\qquad a_i(u)=\min\{u,(s_i-\tau(u))_+\},(15)
where ∑iai(u)=min⁡{ku,∑imin⁡(u,si)}\sum_i a_i(u)=\min\{ku,\sum_i\min(u,s_i)\}. Its exact extinction time is
u*P=max⁡{s1,∑isik}.\label{eq:extinction} u_*^{\mathrm{P}}=\max\left\{s_1,\frac{\sum_i s_i}{k}\right\}.(16)

Proof. The formula is continuous and piecewise affine. On an interval with a positive threshold, let cc corrections be capped at uu and let jj be strictly between zero and uu. Differentiating their sum kuku gives rate one on the capped modes and rate (k−c)/j(k-c)/j on the tied residual plateau. If j=0j=0, then c=kc=k and only the capped modes move, at rate one. This rate lies in [0,1][0,1]; the selected modes are the largest residuals, and the total rate is kk. Thus the negative derivative is in ∂Sk(X)\partial S_k(X). When the nuclear constraint is inactive, every remaining positive residual decreases at rate one; their number is at most kk. The expression is zero exactly when both u≥s1u\geq s_1 and ku≥∑isiku\geq\sum_i s_i. Uniqueness follows from monotonicity of the subdifferential of the convex function SkS_k. ◻

The crossover is k≈‖X0‖*/‖X0‖opk\approx\lVert X_0\rVert_*/\lVert X_0\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}: below it the nuclear budget gives 1/k1/k improvement; above it the largest mode limits progress. This is more informative than a uniform r/kr/k slogan. The continuous model permits full-rank averages at ties, so (15) is not an exact finite-step formula for a literal rank-kk implementation. It is a convexified small-step model. Equation (13) supplies a finite-step check in an important special case.

If X0=sQX_0=sQ, with QQ square orthogonal, the averaged tied maximizer (k/r)Q(k/r)Q makes the F-flow stay on the ray through QQ. Hence

u*P=rsk,t*F=rsak+br.\label{eq:hybridtimes} u_*^{\mathrm{P}}=\frac{rs}{k},\qquad t_*^{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{rs}{ak+b\sqrt r}.(18)
The low-rank F-family saturates at a time of order r\sqrt r instead of r/kr/k, and changing kk has little effect when k≪(b/a)rk\ll(b/a)\sqrt r. This attains the nuclear-capacity lower bound for that flow.

For S-Fanions the coordinate basis matters. If QQ is a normalized Hadamard matrix, so every entry is ±1/r\pm1/\sqrt r, then sign⁡(Q)=rQ\operatorname{sign}(Q)=\sqrt rQ, and the S-flow on the same ray has

t*S=rsak+bηr3/2.\label{eq:Stime} t_*^{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{rs}{ak+b\eta r^{3/2}}.(19)
Such examples can be constructed at every power-of-two rr using the Sylvester recursion. If instead Q=IQ=I, the denominator is ak+bηrak+b\eta r. The gradient singular spectrum is identical in these two examples, but the sign term behaves differently. Entry density is therefore necessary in an explanation of the S-family. These formulas use tied averaged oracles and continuous time, as stated above.

For r=500r=500, a=ba=b, and the paper’s η=.01\eta=.01, the dense-support crossover scales are approximately 22.4 for F-Fanions and 111.8 for dense S-Fanions with maximal entry uniformity. Gaussian-like entries insert a factor 2/π\sqrt{2/\pi}, giving about 89.2 for the latter. These are model scales, not fitted optimum ranks or predictions of exact threshold times.

When should the next rank help?

At a common point with an exact gradient, let a valid directional quadratic upper model have curvature Ck>0C_k>0 along Dk(G)D_k(G). Its best scalar-step decrease certificate is Sk(G)2/(2Ck)S_k(G)^2/(2C_k). Therefore

Sk+12Ck+1≥Sk2Ck⇔Ck+1Ck≤(1+σk+1Sk)2.\label{eq:marginal} \frac{S_{k+1}^2}{C_{k+1}}\geq\frac{S_k^2}{C_k} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{C_{k+1}}{C_k}\leq \left(1+\frac{\sigma_{k+1}}{S_k}\right)^2.(20)
With Frobenius curvature Ck=LkC_k=Lk, put qk=Sk2/(k‖G‖F2)q_k=S_k^2/(k\lVert G\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2). Then the next rank helps this certificate precisely when
σk+1Sk≥1+1/k−1=1k(1+1/k+1).\label{eq:qthreshold} \frac{\sigma_{k+1}}{S_k}\geq\sqrt{1+1/k}-1 =\frac{1}{k(\sqrt{1+1/k}+1)}.(21)
For σi\sigma_i proportional to i−pi^{-p} and 0≤p<10\leq p<1, SkS_k is asymptotic to a constant times k1−pk^{1-p}, so the large-kk rank profile is proportional to k1−2pk^{1-2p} in the range 1≪k≪r1\ll k\ll r, apart from a normalizing factor independent of kk. Spectra flatter than the p=1/2p=1/2 boundary favor more ranks in this model; faster decay eventually penalizes additional weak modes. This is a statement about the certificate’s spectral term, not a universal optimizer ordering.

Nonmonotone hybrid rank curves even for a flat spectrum

Suppose every singular value of GG equals s>0s>0 and a>0a>0. For exact rank-kk directions, put t=b/at=b/a. The F-Fanion cosine squared is

qkF=(k/r+t)2k+t2+2tk/r.\label{eq:flatFprofile} q_k^{\mathrm F}= \frac{(k/\sqrt r+t)^2}{k+t^2+2tk/\sqrt r}.(22)
As a function of real k>0k>0 this decreases until kmin⁡=tr/(r+2t)k_{\min}=tr/(\sqrt r+2t) and increases thereafter; when kmin⁡<1k_{\min}<1 the admissible ranks are on its increasing part. For r=64r=64, a=ba=b, its values at k=1,5,64k=1,5,64 are respectively 0.56250.5625, 0.3642240.364224, and 11. Thus a low-rank F-hybrid can beat an intermediate rank even though the full-rank hybrid has the best score. The minimum for equal coefficients is near r\sqrt r, the same scale as the dense-support crossover found earlier.

For S-Fanions at a flat Hadamard gradient, the same expression holds with t=(b/a)ηrt=(b/a)\eta r; at a flat diagonal gradient, use t=(b/a)ηrt=(b/a)\eta\sqrt r. These are exact common-gradient scores and do not require averaged tie choices. They illustrate how the interior rank curve changes with the hybrid and the coordinate basis. The scalar step is optimized separately in these scores; an actual fixed learning rate must instead be assessed with the next calculation.

A fixed learning rate asks a different question.

For a quadratic with Hessian operator ℋ\mathcal H, set P=uk+1(H)vk+1(H)⊤P=u_{k+1}(H)v_{k+1}(H)^\top. For any of the three families, let Dk′D_k' be its direction, and let a′=1a'=1 for the plain family and a′=aa'=a for either hybrid. Increasing rank changes the direction by a′Pa'P and its actual one-step loss reduction by

γa′⟨G,P⟩−γ22(2a′⟨Dk′,ℋP⟩+(a′)2⟨P,ℋP⟩).\label{eq:fixedmarginal} \gamma a'\langle G,P\rangle -\frac{\gamma^2}{2} \left(2a'\langle D_k',\mathcal H P\rangle +(a')^2\langle P,\mathcal H P\rangle\right).(23)
When H=GH=G and ℋ=LI\mathcal H=L I, the plain criterion is simply σk+1>Lγ/2\sigma_{k+1}>L\gamma/2. For the F-family it is σk+1(1−Lγb‖G‖F)>Lγa2,\sigma_{k+1}\left(1-\frac{L\gamma b}{\lVert G\rVert_{\mathrm F}}\right) >\frac{L\gamma a}{2}, assuming a>0a>0 and a positive parenthesis. For the S-family the cross term also contains ⟨sign⁡(G),P⟩\langle \operatorname{sign}(G),P\rangle. Momentum can make ⟨G,P(H)⟩\langle G,P(H)\rangle differ from σk+1(G)\sigma_{k+1}(G) or even become negative. This equation identifies the exact measurements needed for every new rank.

For the manuscript’s quadratic, ℋD=MDN\mathcal H D=MDN and Ck=∑i,j≤k(ui⊤Muj)(vi⊤Nvj)C_k=\sum_{i,j\leq k}(u_i^\top M u_j)(v_i^\top N v_j). The cross terms are real and need not all be positive. Assuming curvature is just kk times the first-mode curvature is an additional modeling assumption.

Safe intervals from the existing three norms

For 1<k<r1<k<r, write a0=‖G‖opa_0=\lVert G\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}, S=‖G‖*S=\lVert G\rVert_*, Q=‖G‖F2Q=\lVert G\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2, n=r−1n=r-1, p=k−1p=k-1, q=r−kq=r-k, A=S−a0A=S-a_0, V=Q−a02V=Q-a_0^2. Then a0+Lk≤Sk≤a0+Uka_0+L_k\leq S_k\leq a_0+U_k, where h−=pa0+A−(pa0+A)2−4pV2.h_-=\frac{pa_0+A-\sqrt{(pa_0+A)^2-4pV}}{2}.

Lk=max⁡{pA/n,A−qa0,0,h−},Uk=min⁡{pa0,A,pAn+pqn(V−A2n)}.\label{eq:momentbounds} \begin{aligned} L_k&=\max\{pA/n,\ A-qa_0,\ 0,\ h_-\},\\ U_k&=\min\left\{pa_0,\ A,\ \frac{pA}{n}+\sqrt{\frac{pq}{n}\left(V-\frac{A^2}{n}\right)}\right\}. \end{aligned}(24)
These intervals are valid but need not be the tightest possible. For their proof, put H=∑i=2kσiH=\sum_{i=2}^k\sigma_i. Sorting gives H≥pA/nH\geq pA/n and H≥A−qa0H\geq A-qa_0. Cauchy–Schwarz on the two groups gives V≥H2/p+(A−H)2/qV\geq H^2/p+(A-H)^2/q, yielding the upper bound. Conversely the head squares sum to at most a0Ha_0H, and each tail singular value is at most H/pH/p, so V≤a0H+H(A−H)/pV\leq a_0H+H(A-H)/p. Its smaller quadratic root gives the remaining lower bound.

How to turn this into a stronger paper result

A defensible result already available.

Theorem 1, Proposition 3, and Theorem 4 are proved bounds for every rank and hybrid family. The exact flow and finite flat example explain when their rank dependence is attainable. The saved low-rank time comparison and the schedule audit are direct evidence specific to this repository. The short fragment paper_insert_fanions.tex supplies a candidate section without changing the submission.

The most useful next measurement.

At common saved model states, record the actual momentum matrix HH, an independent gradient estimate GG, and the actual implemented directions for each rank. Save Sk(H)S_k(H) and ⟨G,Dk(H)⟩\langle G,D_k(H)\rangle for every desired kk; three aggregate norms cannot replace them. For S-hybrids also record ‖H‖1,entry\lVert H\rVert_{1,\mathrm{entry}} and its sign cross terms. Estimate directional curvature and pairwise cross terms using Hessian-vector products or controlled loss probes at several step lengths. Evaluate both the frozen-state score [⟨G,D⟩]+2/C(D)[\langle G,D\rangle]_+^2/C(D) and the actual scheduled decrease γ⟨G,D⟩−γ2C(D)/2\gamma\langle G,D\rangle-\gamma^2C(D)/2.

This would test a pre-specified rank curve, including the marginal condition (23), instead of assuming that its endpoints determine the interior. Validation accuracy still needs a generalization argument; an optimization convergence bound alone does not imply an accuracy ordering.

Relation to existing literature.

The support-function/descent and best singular-approximation ingredients are standard. Crawshaw et al. develop a norm-based view that also makes layerwise aggregation and update normalization explicit. Ma, Zhang, and He analyze Schatten ball and regularization oracles with momentum, minibatching, and multiple blocks. Their reported stationarity uses the corresponding dual Schatten norm; comparing methods on a common Frobenius target requires retaining norm conversions and structural constants. Our sets CkC_k and their hybrid Minkowski sums make the manuscript’s discrete rank parameter explicit. We make no priority claim for the general analysis or a claim that the cited work establishes a universal Fanion ordering. The first note discusses the other related Muon and structured-optimization papers.
